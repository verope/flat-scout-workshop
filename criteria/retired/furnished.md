## What this measures
Whether the flat comes furnished. Iris and Sam are forming one household from
two, from the end of October, and an unfurnished flat means buying or moving a
flat's worth of furniture into it. This counts for a lot: it is worth about as
much as the aspect and about as much as the building being warm.

It is not a veto. An excellent unfurnished flat is still an excellent flat, and
furniture is a cost like any other — it is priced against what the flat offers,
in the way the brief prices everything else.

## Rubric
**10** — furnished, or fully furnished.
**8** — "furnished or unfurnished, landlord is flexible". A good answer, but an
offer rather than a fact, so it is worth confirming.
**5** — part furnished. Something is there; what, and whether it is the
expensive half, is a question.
**0** — unfurnished. Zero is the bottom of this axis and nothing more: this
Criterion carries no veto, and an unfurnished flat that is excellent everywhere
else still outscores a furnished one that is not.

## Evidence, and what not to read into it
`furnished` is the Portal's own lettings field, not a reading of the prose, and
it is one of the best-covered facts in the whole brief: most Listings carry
one. Where it is absent this is unknown and skips — an advert that says nothing
about furnishing is not an unfurnished flat.

Nothing here is inferred from photographs. A furnished-looking picture is the
current tenant's furniture as often as the landlord's.
